# The launch animation

*Loaded when work touches `lib/src/features/launch/`. The root `CLAUDE.md` stays the entry point and carries the rules that apply everywhere; this file holds the argument behind this directory alone.*

The app opens by assembling its own logo. The composition is the handoff's `patra-launch.jsx`, transcribed beat for beat into `lib/src/features/launch/launch_composition.dart` — a `LaunchStage` that is a pure function of authored time, so the timeline can be read and pinned without a widget tree. Five beats over **7.8s**: the accent stem grows out of the ink (1.1s), five blades are **turned like pages** left to right, staggered 0.26s apart (2.3s), the wordmark rises and its accent period pops (1.4s), the frond breathes once and holds at 1.04 (0.8s), then it flies into the header logo slot while the app fades and rises up behind it (2.2s). The numbers in that file are the design's, not ours to round; the eases are its four (`easeOutCubic` arriving, `easeOutBack` springing, `easeInOutCubic` turning a page, `easeInOutQuart` leaving).

A page turn is the interesting one: a blade does **not** grow in place. It starts flat out at -72° — the same place for all five — swings to its own angle in the fan, and is drawn narrower while it travels (`0.18 + 0.82·turn^0.8`), so it foreshortens as it crosses the spine and has its width back by the time it lands. That is what makes five identical sweeps read as pages rather than as a flower opening.

`LaunchAnimation` wraps the **whole app** from `MaterialApp.builder` rather than being a route, because the last beat needs the frond flying *and* the app it is flying into laid out underneath. Keeping the app mounted from the first frame also means its first requests are made and usually answered while the splash plays, so the shelves that fade up are real rather than skeletons. Three consequences worth knowing:

- **The splash is outside every `Material` in the app.** Text there falls back to Flutter's error style, and since the wordmark's own style sets a size and a colour but no `decoration`, the one attribute that leaks through is a yellow double underline. The `Material(type: transparency)` around it is what supplies a real `DefaultTextStyle`; `test/launch_animation_test.dart` pins it, because the symptom is easy to reintroduce and looks like a font problem rather than a missing ancestor.
- **The flight is aimed at measured slots, not at numbers.** A `LaunchSlot` is a registry the screens put themselves into (`LaunchLogoSlot`, `LaunchWordmarkSlot`), looked up by render box rather than by a global key handed down: three screens carry slots now — the home header, and the two gate screens through `PatraMasthead` — and a route transition can have two of them mounted for a frame, which a single global key answers with a crash. The frond's *bounds* are flown onto the mark slot's rect — the tile it is painted on is larger than the mark, whose fan leaves the tile's top corners empty, so anything that measures the tile lands the mark high. Where no slot is on screen the frond simply fades where it stands.
- **Everything is measured through the frame that is actually laid out**, which is the one *before* the tick doing the measuring. `_painted` keeps the stage the last build used, and its offsets come back off every rect: a slot lives inside the app and is measured through the app's rise, the splash's own wordmark is not in the app but has a rise of its own from the beat it arrived on. Without those corrections the frond lands 18pt low and the wordmark 14pt low — at 60fps a fraction of a pixel, in a test that pumps one big frame the whole rise.
- **The outro adapts to the screen it lands on, not to a flag.** The composition fades the wordmark out at the handoff, because the home header draws the word at 22pt and a shrinking 40pt word would only cross over a different one. The **gate screens draw it at the splash's own 40pt**, so they offer a `LaunchWordmarkSlot` and the word does not resize at all: it travels, and the splash's lockup simply becomes the screen's. Nothing in the animation asks whether a server is configured, or which gate this device landed on — the presence of the slot is the question and the answer, so the outro is right on any screen that offers one and needs no second condition to keep in step. That is what made adding the picker cost the animation nothing. `PatraWordmark` is one definition of the word for that reason: the launch animation *lands* its own on the other two, and tracking that drifted between them would show as a shift at the one moment they are compared directly.

A tap sends the splash home — it seeks to the last beat and lands in 500ms rather than cutting the animation out from under the app — and `MediaQuery.disableAnimations` skips it outright, since someone who asked the OS for less motion is not asking to watch a logo assemble itself for eight seconds. The splash is created once, at app start, and takes itself out of the tree when done: a resume is not a launch. **Nor is a handover**, and that one had to be said in a provider rather than left to the tree: entering another profile builds the whole app again (`SessionScope`), so the animation would be mounted a second time and unfurl the frond over somebody who has just tapped their own face. `isLaunchProvider` is false in every container after the first, and `LaunchAnimation` then never starts its controller at all — which is also why `dispose` only disposes one where there was something to play: a `late final` runs its initialiser at the first read, and an `AnimationController` asks its vsync for a ticker, which asks an element on its way out of the tree for its `TickerMode`.

`lib/src/widgets/patra_frond.dart` is the mark itself, shared by the animation and the header, with the geometry in the SVG's own 88x88 tile space so this file, `res/drawable/patra_mark.xml` and the icon bitmaps can be read against one another. `PatraFrond` is sized by the **mark**, not by the tile. Parchment (`#E9E2D0`) is private to that file and has no token in `theme.dart`: it is brand-mark-only and must never appear in the UI.

The **gate screens carry the same lockup** at their own scale — mark `40 × 24/22` — so the two are the same thing at two sizes rather than two arrangements. It is one widget (`PatraMasthead`), because the picker and the sign-in form are compared directly by the outro landing on whichever is up, and a spacing that drifted between them would show as a shift at exactly that moment. Its gap is the one number that does *not* scale: 0.65em against the header's 0.81em, because spacing that reads as one lockup at 22pt opens into a gulf when the same ratio is taken to 40pt. What is being held constant is that the mark and the word read as one thing, not the ratio that achieves it. **The header draws the five-blade fan at 24pt, not the compact one.** The design's own mock puts a three-blade mark at ~16pt there, and that was measured against a mock whose wordmark is 17px; at the app's 22pt wordmark the same lockup gives a mark that reads as a plain arrow, and the animation then spends two seconds unfurling five blades only to crossfade them into three. The size rule is real — five blades *do* close up at 16pt — but in the app the mark is painted rather than rasterised, so the answer is to draw it big enough for the fan to stay open. 24pt is where it does, and it fits an AppBar's 56pt with room. The gap to the wordmark is 0.81em of the wordmark, which is the mock's lockup spacing.
